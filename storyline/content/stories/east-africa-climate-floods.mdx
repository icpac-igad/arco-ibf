<Block>
  <Prose>
    ## What is Impact-Based Forecasting?

    Traditional forecasts tell you **what the weather will be**.
    Impact-based forecasts tell you **what the weather will do**.

    Instead of saying "200mm of rain expected," an impact-based forecast says:
    "Flash flooding likely in Mathare and Kibera — expect road closures, building collapse
    risk, and waterborne disease outbreak within 48 hours."

    This shift from hazard to impact requires combining weather data with
    satellite imagery, population maps, and infrastructure vulnerability data.
  </Prose>
</Block>

<Block>
  <Prose>
    ## Key Findings: Why Traditional Forecasting Failed
  </Prose>
  <KeyFinding number={1}>
    **The forecast was correct but not actionable.** KMD accurately predicted heavy rain,
    but without impact context, communities didn't know to evacuate. "Heavy rain" means
    different things in different neighborhoods.
  </KeyFinding>
  <KeyFinding number={2}>
    **Exposure data was not integrated.** Weather models didn't account for the 500,000+
    people living in Mathare's floodplain, or the state of the Nairobi Dam, or the
    blocked drainage channels.
  </KeyFinding>
  <KeyFinding number={3}>
    **Warning dissemination reached the wrong people.** Alerts went to government agencies
    and media, but not to informal settlement residents who lacked access to weather apps
    or broadcast media.
  </KeyFinding>
</Block>

<Block>
  <Prose>
    ## The Impact-Based Forecasting Framework
  </Prose>
  <InfoCard title="Hazard: What Will Happen" icon="rain" color="text-blue-400" borderColor="border-blue-500/20" bgColor="bg-blue-500/10">
    Numerical weather prediction models forecast rainfall intensity, duration, and spatial
    distribution. For Nairobi, this means predicting not just totals but intensity per hour —
    critical for flash flood timing.
  </InfoCard>
  <InfoCard title="Exposure: Who Is in the Way" icon="users" color="text-amber-400" borderColor="border-amber-500/20" bgColor="bg-amber-500/10">
    Satellite-derived population maps, building footprints, and infrastructure databases
    identify exactly who and what is in the flood path. Mathare's 500,000 residents on a
    river floodplain represent extreme exposure.
  </InfoCard>
  <InfoCard title="Vulnerability: How Bad Will It Be" icon="alert" color="text-red-400" borderColor="border-red-500/20" bgColor="bg-red-500/10">
    Building materials, drainage capacity, health facility access, and historical flood
    damage data determine how severely a community will be affected. Iron-sheet homes
    in Mathare are far more vulnerable than concrete buildings in Upper Hill.
  </InfoCard>
</Block>
